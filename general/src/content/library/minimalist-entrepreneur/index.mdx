## My review

*The great book on how to do more with less. It’s inspiring and well-written but the most important, is practical and useful. Being in the search for the material for my personal framework on how to start and evolve small businesses, I personally have found clear instructions and valuable insights in this book. ## Synopsis

Sahil Lavingia's book provides a practical guide for entrepreneurs who want to build businesses on a small scale that prioritize sustainability and personal fulfillment over high growth. The focus is on building strong relationships with customers and communities, starting small, and scaling thoughtfully. Lavingia emphasizes that being a creator should be accessible to everyone, and that building a company can be more rewarding when done thoughtfully and sustainably.

## Main Ideas

### 1. Start Small and Validate with Customers
> "Launches are alluring, but they are one-off events I wouldn’t bet your business on. Instead, wait until you have a product with repeat, paying customers. Then launch by thanking them!"

Lavingia warns against putting too much weight on the initial product launch. The real value comes from having a product that customers continuously pay for. Building customer relationships is key, and a product should only be launched when there's something worth celebrating—a group of loyal customers.

### 2. Direct Sales and Customer Engagement
> "Selling your product (or process) directly to customers may seem slow, but it is worthwhile. It will lead to a much better product because the sales process will be less about convincing and more about discovery."

Selling directly to customers allows you to learn more about their needs and develop a better product. This process is less about pushing the sale and more about understanding what your customers want, making it a discovery process.

### 3. Focus on People, Not Companies
> "People don’t care about companies, they care about other people. And you’ve built something from nothing. You love what you do."

Lavingia highlights the importance of personal connection. Customers aren't just buying a product; they're engaging with a person and their values. Therefore, being authentic and sharing personal experiences and insights with the audience can be powerful for building relationships.

### 4. Social Media Strategy
> "Too many people think their business account is enough. No, it’s not. People don’t care about your business and its success, they care about you and your struggles."

This idea connects with the previous point. On social media, it’s more important to share personal stories and struggles rather than just promoting a business. Audiences want to connect with the real person behind the company, making authenticity crucial for building a loyal customer base.

### 5. Scaling Your Business Thoughtfully
> "Repeat customers mean that your business is able to persist without ongoing sales efforts so you can start to focus on scaling."

Once a product has repeat customers, it's time to start scaling, but in a measured way. Lavingia emphasizes that scaling should be done by first growing customer acquisition efforts, then the company, and then personal ambition.

### 6. Building Company Culture
> "Ultimately, it will be more work to build your company culture than your product. But it will also be more valuable."

Lavingia suggests that creating a strong company culture is even more difficult—and more important—than building the product itself. A strong company culture attracts great employees, and those employees are key to improving the company’s vision and execution.

### 7. Fit and Flexibility in Hiring
> "Fit is two-way: If it’s not working out for you, it’s probably not working out for them. Have the hard conversations early, as they’ll only get harder the longer you wait."

Hiring is about finding a mutual fit. If an employee isn’t the right fit for your company, it’s better to address the situation early. This ensures that both parties can move forward in a positive direction.

### 8. Embrace Change in Your Company
> "Your company is a business, not a cult. Embrace change, don’t abhor it."

Lavingia argues that businesses need to remain flexible and open to change. A rigid adherence to the past can hold a company back, whereas an openness to new ideas and change can lead to growth and improvement.

### 9. Focus on Long-Term Impact
> "Never mind if your business isn’t 'changing the world' from day one, or doesn’t employ hundreds of people. As long as you’re making the world better in an honest, scalable way by selling a product worth paying for to a community that wants it, starting a company is worth it."

Starting a business doesn’t have to be about changing the world in a dramatic way. It’s more important to focus on building something meaningful that improves people's lives in a sustainable way. Even if a business starts small, it can still make a significant impact over time.

## Checklist for Building a Company Following Sahil Lavingia's Approach

1. **Start Small and Validate**
   - [ ] Do you have a MVP (manual valuable process) that people are willing to pay for repeatedly?
   - [ ] Have you developed relationships with early customers?
   - [ ] Have you gathered feedback from real customers before launching publicly?
   - [ ] Is your product at a stage where you're ready to thank your paying customers at launch?

2. **Engage Directly with Customers**
   - [ ] Are you selling your product directly to customers?
   - [ ] Are you learning from customers' feedback and adapting your product based on their needs?
   - [ ] Are you viewing the sales process as an opportunity for discovery, not just convincing?

3. Build Personal Relationships, Not Just a Brand
   - [ ] Are you being genuine and transparent in your communications with your customers?
   - [ ] Do you share personal insights, struggles, and learnings as part of your business story?
   - [ ] Are you focusing on building relationships with people, not just promoting a company?

4. **Utilize Social Media Effectively**
   - [ ] Have you created personal and business social media accounts?
   - [ ] Are you sharing personal experiences and stories rather than only promoting your business?
   - [ ] Are you engaging with your audience and responding to their comments?

5. **Scale Thoughtfully**
   - [ ] Do you have repeat customers who provide consistent business?
   - [ ] Is your business ready to scale without needing constant sales efforts?
   - [ ] Are you prioritizing customer acquisition, followed by scaling your company and your ambition?

6. **Build Strong Company Culture**
   - [ ] Have you articulated your company’s core values clearly?
   - [ ] Are you hiring employees who align with your company culture and values?
   - [ ] Are you spending time cultivating your company’s culture to match your long-term vision?

7. **Prioritize Fit in Hiring**
   - [ ] Are you hiring employees who are better than you in their respective roles?
   - [ ] Are you willing to let go of employees who are not a good fit early on?
   - [ ] Do you have clear expectations for the type of company culture you want to create?

8. **Embrace Flexibility and Change**
   - [ ] Are you open to changes in your company’s direction and structure?
   - [ ] Are you avoiding rigidity and cultivating a mindset that embraces innovation and growth?
   - [ ] Are you willing to evolve your vision and adapt as needed to improve?

9. **Focus on Long-Term Impact**
   - [ ] Is your business contributing positively to the world, even on a small scale?
   - [ ] Are you focused on building a sustainable company that solves real problems?
   - [ ] Do you have a community that values your product and is willing to pay for it?
